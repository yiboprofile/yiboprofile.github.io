Five things I built, from a free skin app to a coupon company paid only on results. Each one is a promise to a stranger, and the numbers are whether I kept it.

Why I build

00Why I build

A price is a promise.

I used to wait until I was sure before I went first. In ninth grade I put a price on something I had made for the first time: $2 a month for a trip planner. The first person who paid was planning a real trip to Florida. She did not care that a ninth-grader had built it; she cared that the checklist worked. More than ten people have paid since. What makes a stranger decide to go first is the question behind everything on this page.

Every product here is a different promise to a stranger. Dermat AI costs nothing, so every answer says it is not a diagnosis. SoundSplit charges by the minute: each minute costs me a few cents of compute, and under a subscription the heaviest users would cost more than they paid. Our tutoring charges $37 a session, about half the going rate, because the hour is not what a family is paying for; the plan around it is. Crave Deals takes a commission only on diners we actually bring in, so a restaurant we bring no one to owes us nothing. The numbers under each one are whether I kept the promise.

The economics I have met so far begins after millions of people have already decided: months of FOMC press conferences at the Economics Challenge, a Chipotle case at Wharton. I want to find the part that studies the first decision, the one a person makes before there is any data. My products gave me the questions. I don’t yet have the tools.

What I won’t do: hide a number because it is small, charge a restaurant for diners we didn’t bring, or let someone who paid nothing mistake an answer for a diagnosis. Zero restaurants have signed with Crave Deals so far. That is on this page too.

Dermat AI. No price at all.

Price$0nothing to buy inside
Downloads1,996in 15 months, two stores

An iPhone and Android app for looking after your skin. You scan a photo and it shows what it may be and how sure the model is. There’s also a skin diary, a routine built for your skin and an AI assistant you can ask at any hour. It’s free, with nothing to buy inside.

dermatai.com On the App Store On Google Play

What I learnedGiving Dermat AI away reached about four times as many people a month as SoundSplit, which charges for minutes. People who pay nothing still trust the answer, though, so every answer ends with a note that it is not a diagnosis.

When can you tell if it was right?
  1. Search goodYou can judge it before you buy.e.g. a T-shirt
  2. Experience goodYou can judge it after you use it.e.g. a restaurant meal
  3. Trust goodYou may never judge it yourself.e.g. a diagnosisA skin check sits here

Why it mattersA skin check is a trust good: you can’t tell whether the answer was right until someone qualified tells you, so what a person will pay depends on what they believe about who made it. What does $0 say about a health app from a student team? In the app, a button at the top of the assistant opens the full notice, and every answer has a flag to report it as wrong.

SoundSplit. Sold by the minute.

  • Downloads854606 App Store, 248 Google Play, in 27 months
  • Price$14.99–$119.99a block of minutes; free to download

An iPhone and Android app that splits a song into its stems, so you can take out the vocals for karaoke or mute the piano to practise along. I charge by the minute because every minute I process costs me a few cents of compute. I could see that number, so I priced the number. With a subscription, the heaviest users would cost more than they paid.

soundsplitai.com On the App Store On Google Play
What a block of minutes costs
  • BASIC$14.9950 minutes · $0.30 a minute · 4 bought it
  • STANDARD$34.99150 minutes · $0.23 a minute · 3 bought it
  • PREMIUM$119.99800 minutes · $0.15 a minute · 2 bought it

The more minutes you buy, the less each one costs: from $0.30 down to $0.15. Buyers spent $404.91 in all, before the stores’ fees.

What I learnedMost buyers started small: four of the nine chose Basic. But the two Premium buyers brought in 59% of the money and bought 71% of the minutes, at my lowest price per minute.

What I can’t tell yetI can’t yet tell whether the banner pushes anyone to buy, because I never tracked how many ads a person saw before paying. Each free user earned at most about ten cents in ads, while each of the 9 buyers spent about $45, so one person who paid was worth roughly four hundred who didn’t.

What I’d test nextThe next thing to check is whether $0.15 still covers what a minute costs me to run.

08About

Economics, without an app.

Yibo (Alan) Wang. Urbana High School, Frederick, Maryland, Class of 2027. Economics is what I want to study. My products gave me questions I can’t answer with the data I have; these are the ones I’d bring with me.

  1. Which side of a two-sided market has to go first?Diners won’t come to Crave Deals without deals, and restaurants won’t post deals without diners. Forty pitches have asked the restaurant to go first.
  2. Does $0 raise trust, or lower it?Dermat AI is free and people trust it anyway. 35% of its Android installs are in India, and I don’t know what a free skin check means where a dermatologist is far away.
  3. Does a price that tracks my cost change how people use a thing?SoundSplit’s two Premium buyers bought 71% of all the minutes, at my lowest price per minute.
  4. What are families paying for, the price or the plan?Every tutoring client got both at $37 a session, so I can’t separate them yet.
  5. Does the first decision look the same at the Fed’s scale?At the Economics Challenge I spent months on FOMC press conferences and argued policy as Keir Starmer. I could see the aggregate, and not one person in it.

Where I’ve met economics so far

Economics Challenge, Mount St. Mary’s, 2026 · 1st place

Months of FOMC press conferences, housing market conditions and administrative actions. Each of us played a world figure; I chose former UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer because I knew little about him, and argued his policy recommendations before the university’s economics professors.

Wharton, Leadership in the Business World, 2026

Three weeks of Harvard Business School cases with Wharton faculty. The one I still think about is our own: Chipotle’s expansion into Asia. We priced to match the local chains. I now think the price should have carried the sourcing promise, or the promise should have been dropped.
